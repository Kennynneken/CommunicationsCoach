# The Eight Dimensions

Every report carries a headline score **and** a dimension profile. The headline
score is the rubric-weighted verdict for that context. The dimensions are the
diagnostic underneath it — they say *which channel* is carrying the take and
which one is dragging it down. **The headline is not the average of the
dimensions**; a take can post an 88 in one channel and still score 74 overall.

Same 1–100 calibration as the headline: 100 is historic-best, 40–65 is where
competent everyday communicators live. Score each dimension independently — the
temptation is to let one great line lift all eight, and that destroys the
diagnostic value.

Research anchors per dimension are in `coaching/research-foundations.md`;
citations below are shorthand (Giang, RDJ, Smithyman, Duhigg/study names).

---

### 1. Confidence
*Certainty of delivery and of claim.*
Does he sound like a man making statements, or asking permission? Proxies:
`sentence_final_drop_pct` (Giang: downward endings read as believable; upward
as uncertain), `trail_off_sentences_pct` (trail-off = running out of air/
conviction; keep energy first word to last), volume adequacy (Giang's 1–10
scale: most people default to 3, target 5 — low volume only as a *deliberate*
intimacy tool), filler rate, and — most important — the absence of
self-cancellation ("jokes aside," "shall we," "kind of," "I mean"). A hedge
attached to a strong line is a confidence failure even when the voice is
steady. **Hedges, padding and approval-checks are safety behaviors
(Smithyman): they exist to pre-soften judgment, they raise anxiety, and they
block connection. Name them as such in reports.**
**100:** every sentence lands as something he would defend out loud.

### 2. Warmth
*Does the other person feel liked.*
Warmth is the approach currency: **"Warmth is reflected — put it out and it
comes back" (Smithyman). Withholding it and expecting others to engage is
asking them to take the risk.** Proxies: `smile_pct` and Duchenne proportion,
**smile timing** — the face must be in the emotion *while the words are
spoken*, because "the face is the remote control for the emotion under the
words" (Giang); a warm expression arriving after the sentence delivered the
warmth too late for the sentence to carry it. Also melodic register,
generosity of attention, absence of transactional framing. Warmth is not
niceness — it's whether they leave feeling better about themselves than when
they arrived.
**100:** they feel chosen, not processed.

### 3. Body Language
*Posture, gesture, stillness under pressure, and cross-channel congruence.*
Proxies: `uprightness_mean` **and its trend**, `sway_score`, incongruence
flags, and `gesture_active_pct` — **0% gesture is a deficit, not neutral**
(Giang: gestures live in the Power Sphere, belly-button to eyes, and must
*depict content*; a static body while telling a story is "reporting, not
reliving"). Distinguish the two stillness cases: stillness under pressure or
while listening = composure (score up); stillness while storytelling or
delivering energy = a body left out of the message (score down). Meaningless
repetitive gestures ("visual clutter") score down too.
**100:** the body says the same thing as the words, and says it standing up.

### 4. Presence
*The felt sense of being fully here and unhurried.*
**Rewritten per research — this is about contrast and silence-comfort, not a
target speed.** No expert prescribes a WPM zone for conversation; the failure
mode is a *default* rate ("the moment something becomes default it becomes
non-functional" — Giang), and fast-with-variation is a legitimate elite
profile (RDJ: fast uptake, unhurried key beats). Proxies, in order:
1. **Pause function** — after his own question (does he let it land, or bury
   it?), before a key line, replacing a filler. `deliberate_count` and the
   pause map are the instrument; the scored thing is placement and comfort.
2. **Rate contrast** — does the tempo ever change on purpose? A take at one
   speed throughout scores low at ANY speed. Slow-downs on the beats that
   matter are the tell of command.
3. `silence.lead_in_s` — the pre-turn hold (Giang's Strategic Pause: receive,
   breathe, refine, collect, answer). A genuine strength of his; score it up.
4. Letting the other person finish; not answering his own questions.
**100:** he is the calmest object in the room and silence does not frighten him.

### 5. Verbal Acuity
*Precision, economy, and craft of language.*
Is the word the right size for the thing it describes? Minimizers on heavy
content ("a little bit of family history" for a business collapsing) are the
classic failure. So is the padding clause. So is the adjective stack. Specific
beats general (Giang: senses + emotion; "For Example" — a concrete picture
beats an abstraction, Smithyman). Economy: RDJ's script-breaking lines are
*short*. **100:** every word is the right word and there are no extra ones.

### 6. Unexpectedness
*The Robert Downey Jr. axis — now specified from the verified literature.*
Not randomness — **calculated script-breaking in small doses, on top of full
command of the expected script** ("strategic nonconformity" / "script
transcendence"). The engine is **self-amusement**: say the thing that genuinely
entertains YOU, without checking for approval after ("Do what amuses you…
be the first to bring fun into a situation" — Houpert). Markers: the angle
nobody else in the room would take; a true-but-non-obvious answer where the
stock answer was available; visible enjoyment of his own material *while
delivering it* (smile/pitch spike during, not after); teasing that goes at
himself first, with warmth repair after. Autopilot scores in the 20s no matter
how smoothly it's delivered. This is the scarcest dimension and the one with
the highest ceiling.
**100:** they retell it to someone else that week.

### 7. Curiosity
*Quality and depth of attention on the other person.*
Proxies: **follow-up questions built from their exact words** (Huang et al.:
follow-ups are the single most likability-predictive question type — they
prove listening), layers reached per thread (three-layer test), question
classification — facts of life vs. **feelings about life** (Duhigg: deep
questions ask about values, beliefs, judgments, experiences; "What made you
decide…" not "Where did you…"). Curiosity is also his anxiety tool: attention
on the other person is the clinical cure for self-focus (Smithyman — "don't
try to be interesting; be INTERESTED").
**CEILING RULE (new):** an all-questions take caps at ~70. Pure question-asking
builds liking but not closeness (Smithyman's gap; Aron: no alternation → no
bond). World-class curiosity *braids* questions with self-disclosure — see
Attunement and the reciprocity check.
**100:** they say "nobody has ever asked me that."

### 8. Attunement
*Reading and joining the other person's actual conversation, in real time.*
Now scored in three parts (Duhigg):
1. **Type detection — the Matching Principle.** Every exchange is practical
   ("what's this about?"), emotional ("how do we feel?"), or identity ("who
   are we?"). Did he detect which conversation she was having and join THAT
   one? Answering an emotional bid with a practical response (or vice versa)
   is the canonical miss, even when the words are good.
2. **Mood + energy matching.** Two axes — valence and energy — read from pace,
   posture, volume. Meet them there first, then lead (Giang's
   match-mirror-lead: they're at 4, you start at 4, move to 5). Laughter is
   the sharpest probe: matched intensity = connection accepted; the polite
   chuckle = declined (NASA/McGuire; Provine: most laughter is a bid, not a
   joke verdict).
3. **Looping.** Repeat back their meaning in his own words and check it
   ("So it sounds like X — is that right?"). Step 3 — the check — is the part
   everyone skips and the part that does the work (proof of listening →
   reciprocal listening).
**Reciprocity check (scored here, jointly with Curiosity):** disclosure begets
disclosure only when it alternates (Aron). Track the braid: their share → his
follow-up OR his matching share → theirs. All-questions = interrogation;
all-statements = monologue. Both cap.
**100:** he moves with them before they've announced the move.

## Using the profile

- **Report all eight, every time**, as a compact table with a one-line note on
  the highest and the lowest.
- **Track the flat line.** The dimension that never moves across a session is
  usually the real bottleneck, and it is rarely the one that feels worst in the
  moment.
- **Watch for the trade.** A take that spikes one dimension while another
  collapses (great line, folding posture) is not progress — it's a channel
  robbing another. Name the trade explicitly.
- **THE ONE THING should target the lowest dimension that is blocking the
  others**, not simply the lowest number.
- **One-element rule (Giang):** the follow-up drill fixes ONE element. Never
  assign two.
