# The Eight Dimensions

Every report carries a headline score **and** a dimension profile. The headline
score is the rubric-weighted verdict for that context. The dimensions are the
diagnostic underneath it — they say *which channel* is carrying the take and
which one is dragging it down. **The headline is not the average of the
dimensions**; a take can post an 88 in one channel and still score 74 overall.

Same 1–100 calibration as the headline: 100 is historic-best, 40–65 is where
competent everyday communicators live. Score each dimension independently — the
temptation is to let one great line lift all eight, and that destroys the
diagnostic value.

---

### 1. Confidence
*Certainty of delivery and of claim.*
Does he sound like a man making statements, or asking permission? Proxies:
`sentence_final_drop_pct`, `trail_off_sentences_pct`, filler rate, and — most
important — the absence of self-cancellation ("jokes aside," "shall we," "kind
of," "I mean"). A hedge attached to a strong line is a confidence failure even
when the voice is steady.
**100:** every sentence lands as something he would defend out loud.

### 2. Warmth
*Does the other person feel liked.*
Proxies: `smile_pct` and Duchenne proportion, melodic register, generosity of
attention, absence of transactional framing. Warmth is not niceness — it's
whether they leave feeling better about themselves than when they arrived.
**100:** they feel chosen, not processed.

### 3. Body Language
*Posture, stillness, gesture, and cross-channel congruence.*
Proxies: `uprightness_mean` **and its trend**, `sway_score`, `gesture_active_pct`,
incongruence flags. The trend matters as much as the mean — collapsing 0.12
across a sentence undoes an otherwise upright take.
**100:** the body says the same thing as the words, and says it standing up.

### 4. Presence
*The felt sense of being fully here and unhurried.*
Proxies: `deliberate_count`, `wpm_speaking`, `silence.lead_in_s`, and whether he
lets the other person finish. This is the dimension that silence buys and speed
destroys. Talking fast is not enthusiasm; it is absence.
**100:** he is the calmest object in the room and silence does not frighten him.

### 5. Verbal Acuity
*Precision, economy, and craft of language.*
Is the word the right size for the thing it describes? Minimizers on heavy
content ("a little bit of family history" for a business collapsing) are the
classic failure. So is the padding clause. So is the adjective stack.
**100:** every word is the right word and there are no extra ones.

### 6. Unexpectedness
*The Robert Downey Jr. axis.*
Did anything happen that a competent person could not have predicted? Not
randomness — *inevitable-in-hindsight surprise*: the angle nobody else in the
room would take, the improvised line, the refusal of the obvious script, the
question that isn't in circulation. This is the scarcest dimension and the one
with the highest ceiling, because everything else on this list can be trained to
competence and this one is what separates competent from unforgettable.
Autopilot scores in the 20s no matter how smoothly it's delivered.
**100:** they retell it to someone else that week.

### 7. Curiosity
*Quality and depth of attention on the other person.*
Proxies: layers reached per thread, question classification (informational /
emotional / identity), and whether follow-ups are built from their exact words
or from his own agenda. Distinct from warmth — it is entirely possible to be
warm and incurious, and it reads as pleasant and forgettable.
**100:** they say "nobody has ever asked me that."

### 8. Attunement
*Reading and matching the other person's emotional altitude in real time.*
Not congruence with his own words — congruence with **their** state. Does he
come up when they lift and down when they drop? Does he notice the door opening?
Does he step on a disclosure? Attunement is what makes the difference between a
technically correct question and one that lands.
**100:** he moves with them before they've announced the move.

---

## Using the profile

- **Report all eight, every time**, as a compact table with a one-line note on
  the highest and the lowest.
- **Track the flat line.** The dimension that never moves across a session is
  usually the real bottleneck, and it is rarely the one that feels worst in the
  moment.
- **Watch for the trade.** A take that spikes one dimension while another
  collapses (great line, folding posture) is not progress — it's a channel
  robbing another. Name the trade explicitly.
- **THE ONE THING should target the lowest dimension that is blocking the
  others**, not simply the lowest number.
