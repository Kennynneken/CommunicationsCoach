# Rubric — Dinner Party

100 = the guest everyone hopes to be seated next to; historic-best social warmth.

## Weighting
- Storytelling (relived not reported; pause between ideas; emotional beats) — 30%
- Group dynamics (includes others, sharp questions, leads without dominating) — 25%
- Warmth (smile ON the words, melodic voice, laughter matched) — 20%
- Depth (at least one thread taken 3 layers deep across the evening) — 15%
- Composure & pacing (never rushes; comfortable holding the table) — 10%

## Notes
- Storytelling WPM target 120–150 with a clear pause-between-ideas pattern in
  the pause map (a told story is prepared remarks — the zone applies); flag
  stories delivered > 165 WPM as rushed. **Relive, don't report**: present
  tense, the body in it (gesture_active spiking on beats — a statue telling a
  story is reporting), senses + one emotion, and land the meaning ("the reason
  I'm telling you this…"). 2–3 minutes max, then hand the table back.
- Talk-time in groups: leading is 30–45% in a 4+ person setting, not 60%.
  **The Sievers finding is the calibration: the highest-influence person in a
  group asks 10–20x more questions than average and speaks LESS than the
  dominant talker — and dominant-talker groups bond least.** Leading the table
  = directing attention, not holding the floor.
- Reward callbacks (referencing something someone said earlier) — search the
  transcript for them; they are proof of listening at the group scale, the
  same mechanism as a follow-up question.
- Reward naming a first responder when he asks the group a question ("Sarah,
  you first") — groups stall because nobody wants to go first.
- Laughter matching applies at table scale: when the table laughs, does his
  energy join it at matched intensity, and does he let it finish before
  speaking into it?
