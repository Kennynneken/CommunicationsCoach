## Why it still didn't pass

**He responded to "an ER nurse" instead of to Mike.** Mike's disclosure had
two specifics — sitting in the parking garage not going in, and his wife's
sentence: *"You don't talk about anyone by name anymore."* That second one is
the most intimate thing a stranger has offered in this entire scenario, and
the response touched neither. Instead: "seen some shit," "people who are sick
all day," "things you can never unsee" — the *category's* trauma (gore,
sickness), not the *person's* disclosure (numbness, depersonalization, being
seen by his wife before he saw himself). Mike didn't say anything about what
he saw. He said he stopped feeling. The response empathized with the wrong
wound.

Three "I imagine" clauses make it worse in two directions at once: they hedge
(confidence), and they relocate the conversation into Kenny's speculation
instead of Mike's experience (attunement — perspective-taking instead of
perspective-getting, the exact failure Epley's research names).

"How'd you move forward through it?" is a workable question shape — but on
top of a miss, it reads as coach-speak, and it invites Mike to summarize
rather than to stay in the moment he just opened.
