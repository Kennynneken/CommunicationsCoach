## What landed

- **00:02 — "What was going through your mind in that moment?"** Layer three.
  He asked about her interior at a specific instant, not about the deal, the
  company, or the lesson. This is the deepest question the rubric asks for and
  he found it on a disclosure most people would have flinched from.
- **The two-second hold before speaking, at 0.99 uprightness.** He let

## What to fix

- **00:02 — "That must have been a really eye-opening experience for you."**
  Two errors in one sentence. First, *"must have been"* tells her what she felt
  instead of asking. Second, **"eye-opening" is a performance-review word for a
  moral collision.** She described becoming the villain of her own childhood.
  Calling that eye-opening is the third consecutive minimizer — after "a little
  bit of family history" and "a little bit more" — and it's the one that costs
  the most, because it arrives on the most exposed thing she's said.
- **The throwaway clause migrated from after the question to before it.** Same
  habit, new position: he still cannot let a question stand by itself. "I'm up
  to no good" + *jokes aside*; the layer-2 question + *the name speech*;
  "somewhere real" + *shall we*; and now *the label* + the question.
- **sentence_final_drop 100% → 0%.** Both sentences ended flat or rising on the
  gravest moment of the conversation. When she is that exposed, an upward ending
  reads as unsure whether he can hold what she just handed him.

## THE ONE THING

**Ask the question naked — first word out of your mouth is the question, and
land it with a full stop.** Current: 7 takes, 7 questions, every single one
padded with a clause before or after it, and 0 deliberate pauses. Target next
take: **"What was going through your mind?"** — nothing before it, nothing after
it, voice down at the end. Six words. If he had opened with those six words and
stopped, this take scores in the mid-80s.
