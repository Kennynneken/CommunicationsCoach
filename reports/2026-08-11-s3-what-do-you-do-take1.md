## What to fix

- **00:04 — "Jokes aside."** He undid his own best line. Two seconds after
  landing something memorable he took it back and handed Dana the LinkedIn
  version she'd already braced for. "Jokes aside" is an apology for being
  interesting. Never cancel your own good line — the next sentence should be the
  *proof* of the joke, not the retraction.
- **The real answer is a category, not a scene.** "Chief creative officer…
  execute on huge, fun, exciting creative visions… websites, marketing funnels,
  and materials like that." Three vague adjectives, one title, one deliverables
  list. Nobody can picture anything. Nobody remembers it. Compare: *"Last month
  I talked a guy out of the website he wanted and built him the one he needed —
  he tripled his sales and is still mad about it."*
- **00:19 — "What about you? What do you do?"** He mirrored back the exact
  autopilot question, and stacked two questions again — the same pattern as
  take 1. This was the moment to ask about *her*, not to run the script back.
- **Still no name. Three takes, forty-seven seconds.** This has been THE ONE
  THING twice now. Dana still cannot say his name out loud.

## THE ONE THING

**Delete the retreat sentence. When you land a line like "I'm up to no good,"
the next words out of your mouth are the proof, not the disclaimer — and your
name rides in on it.** Current: best line in the session, cancelled 2.1s later
by "jokes aside," followed by a title and an adjective stack. Target next take:
*"Oh, I'm up to no good. Kenny, by the way."* — beat — then **one concrete story
with one real person in it, under 15 seconds, no job title anywhere in it.**
If the sentence could appear on a business card, it doesn't count.
