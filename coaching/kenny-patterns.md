### 1. The padding clause (the defining habit)
He cannot let a good line stand alone. Every strong sentence gets a second
sentence that explains, softens, tags, or labels it.

| Take | The good line | The padding |
|---|---|---|
| s3 | "Oh, I'm up to no good. That's what I do." | *"Jokes aside…"* |
| s4-layer2 | "Why do you say it was embarrassing?" | *8-second name introduction* |
| s4-layer3-1 | "Let's take this back somewhere real." | *"…shall we?"* |
| s4-layer3-2 | "What was going through your mind?" | *"That must have been eye-opening"* (moved to the front) |

| s15-take3 | "…who or what this problem is." | *"Very curious."* |

Note the migration — when told to stop adding a clause *after*, he added one
*before* ("Seems like" → "Well" → "Hmm. Well" across S15 takes 1–3). The
instinct is the target, not the position.

**Clean takes on record: s12-exit ("I don't try to be good. I try to be real.")
and s15-take4 ("March 11th." / "What happened on March 11th?"). Both are the
best line of their session. Not a coincidence. Both are under twelve words.**

### 2. The relocation reflex — NEW, found 2026-08-13
When the emotional content arrives, he proposes that somebody physically move.
Three for three across S15 takes 1–3:

| Take | Her move | His answer |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Sighs, available | *"Seems like you need some **space**"* |
| 2 | "It's my own fault." | *"Why don't we **go get some warm coffee**"* |
| 3 | "It has a name and a date." | *"Let's **get up, move our bodies around**"* |

In isolation it reads as a bold frame change — take 2 scored 80 on
unexpectedness for exactly this. On the third repetition it is avoidance wearing
the costume of leadership, and it functions as an exit he hands the other
person. **A single-take session would have scored it as a strength.** Only the
four-beat roleplay exposed it. He broke it in take 4 under the strongest pull
yet; watch whether it returns next session.

### 3. Steady vs. small — NEW, watch for a second appearance
S15 take 4: mean intensity **34.9 dB** and `trail_off` **50%**, both first-time
failures against a clean 46.9 / 58.3 / 57.5 dB at 0% across every prior take.
Told to be slow and still, he became *small* — intensity dipped to **18.7 dB**
mid-take (essentially inaudible) before he re-launched for the question. That
dip is him losing his nerve inside his own silence. **Quiet is intimacy; fading
is uncertainty.** Promote to a standing pattern if it repeats.

### 4. Minimizers on heavy content
He shrink-wraps other people's serious disclosures with small words.
- "a little bit of family history" — for a family business collapsing
- "a little bit more about this embarrassment"
- "that must have been a really **eye-opening** experience" — for a described
  moral collision

Related: he nominalizes feelings ("embarrassingly" → "this embarrassment"),
which turns a person into a case study.

### 5. Cold face on a warm line
Smile drops to ~0.0 exactly when the warm or vulnerable sentence lands
(s1 @7.0–9.4s, s4-risk @7.03s, s4-layer3-1 @5.0–6.7s, s15-take1 @3.67–4.0s,
s15-take2 across 11 of 13 frames including the entire invitation, s15-take3
@6.67–8.0s). Six separate takes. **Now the most reliably reproduced habit in the
file.** Worst version: s15-take2, where he invited a stranger to leave the room
with him at 15% smile and a completely flat face.

**Exceptions, and he got both right:** s4-layer3-2 and s15-take4 — flat face on
her disclosure, which is correct congruence, not coldness. He can read an
altitude drop and match it; the open question is whether he can come back up
(s12: she laughed, he stayed at 0% smile with posture collapsing).

### 6. Stacked questions
Asks two questions back to back, so only the second gets answered
(s1-take1, s3). Fewer questions, more silence.

## Native assets — build on these

- **Pitch variance is elite.** Scores of 94, 100, 100, 100, 90; range up to
  67–358 Hz. Melody is his best instrument and he under-uses it when he speeds up.
- **Genuine warmth.** Duchenne proportion is 84–100% of all smiles — when he
  smiles it is never social, always real.
- **Wit under pressure.** "I'm up to no good," "let's take this back somewhere
  real," "I don't try to be good, I try to be real" — all improvised, all
  memorable. The raw material is there; the discipline around it is the work.
- **Callbacks off physical detail.** New and reliable as of 08-13: he builds
  lines from things nobody told him to notice. "Cool cool cool" → *"space to
  cool down"*; a cold coffee cup → *"let's get some warm coffee"*; "it has a
  name and a date" → *"who or what this problem is"*. Three consecutive takes.
  This is a distinct asset from the wit and it is worth feeding.
- **He takes correction and applies it on the very next rep.** Filler, padding,
  tempo (−88 WPM), the first deliberate pause in eleven takes, the relocation
  reflex, hedges and question marks all corrected on the take immediately
  following the note. **The constraint on this repo is picking the right ONE
  THING, not getting him to do it.**
