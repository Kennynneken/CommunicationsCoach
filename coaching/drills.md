# The Drill Library

Every drill the coach can assign, mapped to the dimension it trains and the
research behind it (`coaching/research-foundations.md`). THE ONE THING in any
report should be one of these, made concrete with Kenny's current numbers.
**One element per rep (Giang's one-element rule). Never assign two.**

---

## Approach (the current campaign)

### A1 · The Mediocre First Impression
**Trains:** the approach itself · **Source:** Smithyman
Walk up with the goal set to *stranger → non-stranger*. Nothing clever
required: warmth out, plain introduction, name, one question. The rep fails
only if he performs. Scoring a cold open on brilliance is a category error —
score warmth-on-time, plainness, and whether a door was left open.

### A2 · Social snacks
**Trains:** approach volume, desensitization · **Source:** Smithyman
Daily: one micro-interaction beyond the transactional minimum with a barista,
clerk, stranger in a line. Say one more sentence than necessary, ask one
question. These are approach reps at 1% stakes. Homework between sessions —
not recorded, just counted.

### A3 · The 3 Strangers Rule
**Trains:** room entry · **Source:** Smithyman (his own 20-year practice)
At any event: three plain introductions before any other goal exists. The
first conversation is a warm-up rep; it is not supposed to be the important
one. Controllable factors only: approach, warmth, name, question. Outcomes are
not the metric.

### A4 · Smile-then-speak
**Trains:** warmth timing · **Source:** Giang (face = remote control of tone)
The smile starts half a second BEFORE the first word and holds through the
last word of the warm line — the last word gets more face than the first.
Test on tape: does the pitch data sound different? (It will — a smile is
audible.) His curve currently peaks *after* speech ends; the drill is to move
the existing smile left, not to manufacture one.

## Silence & tempo

### S1 · Ask, then shut up
**Trains:** presence, curiosity yield · **Source:** Duhigg (the question's
value is in the answer), Giang (silence after = confidence)
One question, then closed mouth until the other person has spoken. Count
one-one-thousand-two if needed. The recording must END in his silence — if the
clip ends on his voice, the rep didn't happen. Current record: buried his own
question in 0.64s.

### S2 · The pause replaces the filler
**Trains:** presence, composure · **Source:** Giang (verbatim prescription)
Wherever an "um/like/you know" wants to happen, close the mouth instead. Do
not suppress fillers into faster speech — convert them into silence. (His
filler is already at zero; this drill guards the streak under load.)

### S3 · One deliberate slow-down
**Trains:** rate contrast · **Source:** Giang (contrast, not a zone), RDJ
(fast-but-varied)
Pick the one sentence in the take that matters most and deliver it at half
speed with a beat of silence before it. Everything else can stay at his
natural 210+ — the contrast IS the skill. This replaces the old "get into
110–150" instruction for conversation. (110–150 still applies to prepared
speeches/toasts.)

### S4 · The Strategic Pause (pre-turn)
**Trains:** composure · **Source:** Giang (named technique — receive, breathe,
refine, collect, answer)
Already a native strength (3.09s, 2.45s holds with rising posture). Maintain:
before any turn under pressure, one full breath before the first word.

## Depth & reciprocity

### D1 · The conversion rep
**Trains:** curiosity · **Source:** Duhigg (verbatim examples)
Take the fact-question that wants to come out and convert it before it leaves:
"Where'd you get it" → "What made you pick it." "What do you do" → "Do you
love it?" / "What's eating your week?" Facts of life → feelings about life.
The test: if the best possible answer is one word, the question was wrong.

### D2 · Three layers, their words
**Trains:** curiosity, attunement · **Source:** Huang (follow-ups), the
3-layer test
Every follow-up must contain one of THEIR exact words. Layer 1: their answer.
Layer 2: a question built from its most emotionally loaded word. Layer 3:
the person underneath ("what was going through your mind"). He has reached
layer 3 exactly once (s4-layer3-take2); it produced her best disclosure and
his best attunement score.

### D3 · The braid (BUBBLE answers)
**Trains:** reciprocity — the liking-vs-closeness gap · **Source:** Smithyman
(BUBBLE), Aron (no alternation → no bond)
When asked about himself, the answer must carry one of: **motive** ("because —
we don't care what you did, we care why"), **contrast** ("but" — the thing you
couldn't guess from the outside), **current motion** ("lately" — what he's
*doing*, not what he *does*), or **a concrete picture** ("for example").
One layer below surface: personal, not private. Then hand the turn back.
All-questions caps at liked-not-known; all-statements is a monologue. Alternate.

### D4 · The loop
**Trains:** attunement · **Source:** Duhigg/Heen (looping for understanding)
After a real disclosure: repeat her meaning back in his own words, then CHECK
— "is that right?" The check is the step everyone skips and the step that does
the work. One loop per conversation, at the moment that matters most.

## Unexpectedness & self

### U1 · The non-obvious true answer
**Trains:** unexpectedness · **Source:** RDJ literature (script transcendence)
Once per conversation: where the stock answer exists, give the true-but-
non-obvious one instead. Small doses, full command of the script first. "How's
business?" has a stock answer; "honestly, my AI agents did most of my week's
work and I'm still deciding how I feel about that" is a true door.

### U2 · Self-amusement calibration
**Trains:** unexpectedness, warmth · **Source:** Owen Cook via Houpert
("do what amuses you"), RDJ (visible enjoyment while delivering)
Say the thing that genuinely entertains HIM — and enjoy it *while* saying it
(smile during, pitch spike during), without checking for approval after.
The approval-check afterward is the padding clause's facial twin.

### U3 · Tease self first
**Trains:** unexpectedness, warmth · **Source:** RDJ literature (reparative
humor)
Any tease aimed outward must be preceded somewhere by one aimed at himself,
and followed by warmth repair. Self-deprecation from a base of self-acceptance
reads as confidence; without the base it reads as fishing.

## Body

### B1 · Charades hands
**Trains:** body language · **Source:** Giang (Power Sphere, depict content)
While telling any story: hands between belly button and eyes, and they must
DRAW something — the size, the direction, the number. Zero gesture through a
story is reporting, not reliving. Current `gesture_active_pct`: 0.0 across
all three cold-approach takes.

### B2 · Contained power
**Trains:** presence, energy · **Source:** Giang (12/10 in, 8/10 out)
Before walking into a room: physically amp (breath, movement, music) to a 12,
deliver at an 8. The gap is felt as magnetism. Pairs with A3.

### B3 · Volume to 5
**Trains:** confidence · **Source:** Giang (1–10 scale; default is 3)
One notch louder than feels natural, especially on the first sentence of an
approach. It will feel like shouting; it isn't. Drop volume only ON PURPOSE
(intimacy on the line that matters).

## Economy & exits (Greene block)

### E1 · Say it once
**Trains:** verbal acuity · **Source:** Greene (Law 4: say less than
necessary), the padding-clause work
Deliver the point, then stop — no restatement in weaker words, no trailing
lap. "The more you say, the more common you appear." The padding clause and
the trailing repetition are the same habit at different scales. Test on
transcript: any sentence that re-says the previous one gets cut.

### E2 · Leave at the peak
**Trains:** presence, memorability · **Source:** Greene (Law 16 /
Covetousness: "it is not possession but desire that impels people")
End the conversation on its emotional high point — the laugh, the landed
line, the exchanged real thing — not two minutes into its decay. Close with
specific value + concrete next step, downward ending, gone. In roleplay:
Kenny decides when the scene ends, and is scored on whether he chose the peak.

### E3 · The Mitfreude rep
**Trains:** warmth · **Source:** Greene (Law of Envy, inverted)
When the scene partner shares good news: visible joy, fast — pitch lifts,
face joins, then a follow-up question about THEIR win. No pivot to his own
story (one-upping is the grandiosity tell). Coach injects a good-news beat
into scenes specifically to score this.

### E4 · Gap-reading
**Trains:** curiosity, attunement (elite tier) · **Source:** Greene (Ideal
Lover / victim theory: "people are constantly giving out signals as to what
they lack")
Within three minutes, form one hypothesis about what this person's role
starves them of — recognition, fun, being taken seriously, a real question —
and aim one move at the gap instead of the surface topic. Scored on whether
the hypothesis showed up in his next question.

## Boundaries

### X1 · The playful exit
**Trains:** composure under pressure · **Source:** RDJ walkout analyses
When a conversation turns bad: name the shift lightly, stay warm, leave clean.
Template: "This got a little [X] — I'm going to grab a drink. Come find me if
[Y]." No counter-attack, no apology, downward ending. And per RDJ's own
retrospective: sooner than feels polite.

## Session design rules

1. **Rotate one drill from each block across a session** — approach, silence,
   depth — rather than three reps of one. Interleaving survives contact with
   real rooms better than blocking.
2. **Same-night debrief (S14 in the scenario deck) stays the highest-value
   recording** — unscripted speech under mild fatigue is the honest baseline.
3. **Record & Review protocol for self-study** (Giang): when Kenny reviews his
   own takes, three passes — audio only, then video muted, then transcript.
   One element per week.
4. **Prepared ≠ inauthentic.** Back-pocket questions, pre-planned answers to
   "what do you do," rehearsed exits — all four sources converge: preparation
   is the mechanism of ease ("Don't hope for a good conversation. Prepare for
   one.").
