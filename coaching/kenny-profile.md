# Kenny — Real-World Profile

Ground truth for scenario design. **Read this with `kenny-patterns.md` before
building any scene.** Scenes must run on material Kenny actually has — the one
time a scene required him to invent a fake colleague and a fake project history
(the S13 toast, 2026-08-14) he correctly rejected it as an acting exercise
rather than a communication drill.

Last updated: 2026-08-14 (first profile intake)

---

## What he actually does

Two halves, both genuinely interesting in a room — this is real material he
already owns and does not have to invent.

**1. E-commerce — two brands he owns**
- **Old World Healing** — supplements (NutriCircle space).
- **A Thing Of 50** — creatine for women over 50; more broadly women's
  nutrition, fitness and weight loss.

**2. AI systems**
- Builds AI systems and agents with Claude Code — including agentic marketing
  departments running inside his own businesses.

**Scenario note:** "I build AI agents that run my marketing department" and
"I sell creatine to women over 50" are both memorable, slightly unexpected
answers. He does not need a better job to answer "what do you do" well — the
raw material is already above average. The work is delivery, not content.

## The rooms he's actually in

- **Frequency is low and he wants it higher.** The last few years: one or two
  big business conferences a year, not much else. Named: Four Rooms, Affiliate
  Takeover — affiliate/marketing world.
- **The goal from here: something new every month.** Explicitly *not* the same
  event repeatedly — business events, conferences, business dinners, business
  parties, mixed bag.
- **Geography: Columbus, Ohio area**, plus travel to conferences.

**Scenario note:** build scenes in real Columbus venues and real event types.
Vary the room every session — repeating one venue misses the point of his
stated goal. Conference-floor and after-party scenes are as relevant as local
mixers.

## What he actually loses — ranked

### 1. The approach. This is the whole game.
> "Once I'm in a conversation it's fine, or I'm in a group it's fine, but
> definitely the approach and getting in is the hard part."

**Hardest case: large groups and high-stimulus rooms** — lots of noise, lots
going on, and he has to walk up and start something from nothing.

**This has never been drilled.** All 8 takes of 2026-08-11 began mid-stream,
with the other person already speaking to him. The scored record contains zero
cold approaches. Every future session should include at least one.

### 2. Conversational stalls / surface lock
Threads that stay informational and get awkward. Sometimes recoverable,
sometimes — correctly — a conversation to walk away from. Note he already knows
the difference; don't coach walking away as a failure.

### 3. Approaching women — a mindset residue, not a goal
> "Not that I'm trying to pick them up or anything, but just from childhood
> there are just mindset things there."

**How to handle this in scenes:** put women in scenes as ordinary participants
in business rooms — because they are — and never frame the scene as seduction
or pickup. The target is the approach hesitation, not an outcome with the
person. Do not make it a theme he has to notice; just stop letting the rooms be
all-male, and score the approach the same either way.

---

## Design rules that follow from all this

1. **No invented backstory.** He plays himself, with his real brands, his real
   work, his real opinions. Scenes supply the *room and the other people*, never
   his history.
2. **Start scenes earlier than feels natural.** The rep begins before the first
   word, at the moment he decides to walk over — not once he's already talking.
3. **High-stimulus by default.** Loud rooms, groups mid-laugh, several things
   happening. Quiet two-person high-tops are the easy case and he already
   handles them.
4. **Rotate venues and event types every session**, mirroring the monthly-new-
   event goal.
