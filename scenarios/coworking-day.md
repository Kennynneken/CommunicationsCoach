# Scenario Deck — Coworking Day (Meeting a Stranger Cold)

A deliberately smaller deck than `columbus-business-party.md`. Five cards, one
continuous scene, one person. The mixer deck trains *rooms*; this one trains
**one stranger, no agenda, no name tags, no reason to be talking** — which is
harder, because at a mixer the context does half the work for you.

**Setting:** a coworking space off High Street in the Short North. Tuesday,
11:40am. Not an event. Nobody is here to network. That's the point.

---

## The character — Marisol Vega

- Mid-30s. Been in the building three weeks. Nobody has introduced her to
  anyone.
- **Live wire:** she is carrying a four-foot roll of butcher paper under one
  arm and will not explain it unless asked. It is the most interesting object
  in the room and the autopilot response is to not mention it.
- **How she plays:** warm but self-protective. Rewards a real question with a
  real answer. Answers autopilot questions pleasantly, briefly, and with a
  half-step back toward her laptop.
- **What she is actually doing here:** she left something and hasn't told
  anyone in this building what. Reachable at layer three. Not before.

---

## C1 · The Grinder (cold open)

**Moment:** Shared kitchen. She's fighting the burr grinder one-handed, roll of
paper wedged under the other arm. She catches your eye and says:
*"Don't judge me — I have no idea what I'm doing with this thing."*

**Autopilot (scores 35–45):** Solve the machine. *"Oh, you have to hold the
button down."* Helpful, forgettable, and the conversation is now about a coffee
grinder. Or the equally dead *"Ha, no worries — you new here?"*

**The move:** She handed you a self-deprecating line. Two things are on the
table and the grinder is neither of them: **her**, and **the paper**.
- Match the warmth *first* — a real smile before the sentence, not after.
- Then go at the object nobody else will mention: *"I'm more interested in
  what's under your arm, honestly."*
- Keep it to one sentence. Say it, then stop talking and let her fill it.

**Coach's eye:** articulation rate in the first ten seconds (the cold-open
sprint is the standing tell), whether the smile precedes or trails the warm
line, and whether one sentence was allowed to stand without a padding clause.

**Context tag:** `small-talk`

---

## C2 · The Paper (what she's actually doing)

**Moment:** She tells you what the paper is for. The answer is odd and slightly
self-conscious, and she watches your face while she says it.

**Autopilot:** Evaluate it. *"Oh that's cool"* / *"That's smart."* Assessment
kills disclosure — she now knows you're grading.

**The move:** Curiosity, not verdict. Ask about the *why she does it that way*,
not the *what it is*. And let your face do the reacting so your words don't
have to.

**Coach's eye:** minimizers and nominalizations on her disclosure. Watch for
"a little bit of…" and for turning a feeling into a topic.

**Context tag:** `small-talk`

---

## C3 · The Layer-Three Door

**Moment:** Somewhere in her answer is the crack — the thing she left. She will
open it one inch and no further unless the next question earns it.

**The move:** Every answer contains the next question. If you reach for a new
topic here, you skipped a layer and the door shuts politely.

**Coach's eye:** layer count in the thread, and the pause *before* the question
that goes at the crack. Heavy questions asked quickly read as prying; the same
question after a 1.5s hold reads as care.

**Context tag:** `small-talk`

---

## C4 · The Interruption

**Moment:** Someone she half-knows leans into the kitchen and pulls her
attention for fifteen seconds. You lose the floor mid-thread.

**The move:** Don't restart from zero and don't apologize for existing. Hold
your position, stay still, and when she turns back, return her to the exact
sentence she was in — *"You were about to tell me why you stopped."* Being the
person who remembers is rarer than being the person who's interesting.

**Coach's eye:** posture and stillness during the fifteen seconds you are not
being talked to. This is where confidence leaks invisibly.

**Context tag:** `small-talk`

---

## C5 · The No-Agenda Exit

**Moment:** Your 12:00 is in four minutes. There is no business reason to see
her again and both of you know it.

**Autopilot:** *"Well, I'll let you get back to it."* — the discard line.

**The move:** Name the specific thing, then make the next step small and real.
No pitch, no LinkedIn. *"Same time Thursday? I want to know how the wall turns
out."* Specific and low-stakes beats warm and vague.

**Coach's eye:** sentence-final cadence on the closing line. Rising endings
undo the whole conversation.

**Context tag:** `small-talk`
