# Scenario Deck — Coworking Day / Casual Third Space

Meeting strangers with **no event structure to hide behind**. A mixer gives you
a nametag, a reason to be there, and a socially sanctioned opener. A coworking
floor gives you none of that — approaching someone is a choice you visibly make,
and everyone in the room knows it. That exposure is the whole training value.

## S15 · The Overheard Sigh

**Where:** Second floor of a coworking space off High Street, 2:40pm on a
Tuesday. Long communal table, eight people on laptops, nobody talking. You've
been here since noon and haven't said a word to anyone.

**Who:** A woman roughly your age two seats down. Laptop covered in stickers,
one of which is a logo you don't recognize. Cold coffee next to her. She has
been typing in bursts and stopping for the last twenty minutes.

**Moment:** She closes her laptop harder than she meant to, leans back, exhales,
and says — half to herself, half to the room — *"Okay. Cool. Cool cool cool."*
She is not talking to you. She is also very obviously available to be talked to.
You have about four seconds before she opens the laptop again and the door
closes.

**Autopilot (scores 35–48):**
- Pretend you didn't hear it. Safest, costs nothing, gains nothing.
- *"Ha — rough one?"* delivered at 230 WPM to your own screen, no eye contact.
  A sound, not a sentence.
- Jump to logistics: *"Do you work here a lot?"* / *"What do you do?"* — you
  turned a live emotional moment into an intake form.

**The move:** She handed you a feeling. Take the feeling, not the facts.
- **Turn your body first, then speak.** Chair, shoulders, face — commit to the
  approach physically before a single word. Talking sideways at a screen reads
  as hedging your bet.
- **Smile before the sentence, not after it.** The warmth has to be in the room
  before the words arrive, or it lands as a comment instead of an opening.
- Say one short thing that acknowledges what actually happened, and then
  **stop talking.** *"That was three 'cools.' Two is fine. Three is a story."*
- Let the silence be hers to fill. Do not add a clause. Do not explain the joke.

**The trap:** This card is engineered against the padding habit. There is a
strong pull to soften the observation with a follow-on — *"…sorry, I couldn't
help overhearing,"* *"…not that it's any of my business."* Every one of those
clauses hands the moment back and tells her you weren't sure you were allowed.

**Coach's eye:**
- `wpm_speaking` — target under 160. The whole card fails at 220.
- Whether the utterance is **one sentence or two.** One is the pass.
- `smile` onset relative to speech onset — smile must lead.
- `silence.lead_in_s` — a real breath before speaking is a strength he already
  has; it should be present here.
