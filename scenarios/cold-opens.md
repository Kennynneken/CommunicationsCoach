# Scenario Deck — Cold Opens

Ten situations where **nothing gives you permission to speak**. No event, no
name tag, no host, no shared context, no one who introduced you. Silence is the
default state of the room and the default wins unless you break it.

This is the hard version of the Columbus deck. There, Dana opened the
conversation and the room supplied a reason to talk. Here you are the entire
reason the conversation exists, and if your first eight words are weak the
interaction ends politely and permanently.

**Card numbering is `c<N>`** so cold-open takes stay separate from the
`s<N>` mixer cards in the trend:

```
sessions/YYYY-MM-DD-c<N>-<slug>-take<K>.MOV
```

**How to use a card**
1. Read the *Moment* and the *Autopilot* version. Autopilot is what you do by
   default when your heart rate is up and the window is closing.
2. Record 30–90 seconds running the *Move*. Both sides of the dialogue yourself
   is fine — the metrics scored are yours.
3. Drop the take in `sessions/`, run it with the card's context tag.

**Rubric mapping.** Most cold opens are two-person, seated or standing, no
agenda → `small-talk` (emotional depth, melody, novelty, turn dynamics). A
handful that happen inside a professional frame → `networking`.

---

## THE COLD-OPEN LAW

**You get one sentence to prove you are not a threat, not a salesman, and not
boring.** In that order. Warmth handles the first, specificity handles the
second, novelty handles the third. Autopilot openers fail the third instantly —
and the third is the only one that gets you a second sentence.

Corollary: **comment on the shared moment, not on her.** The weather, the line,
the object in her hands, the absurd thing happening ten feet away. A cold open
about *her* is an evaluation; a cold open about *the moment* is an invitation.

## ACT I — Breaking the Silence

### C1 · The Coffee Shop Wait
**Where:** Fox in the Snow, Italian Village, Saturday 9:40am. Long line, four
people bunched at the end of the counter waiting on drinks.
**Moment:** She's waiting on the same bar you are. Not on her phone — rare
enough to be a signal. You have roughly forty seconds before a name gets
called and the window closes permanently.
**Autopilot (scores 30–40):** Say nothing. Rehearse three openers, discard all
three, take the cortado, leave. Or worse — "so, do you come here a lot?"
**The move:**
- Breath first. You already do this well — 2.4s and 3.1s pre-turn holds with
  posture *rising*. Use it here; it is your best asset in the first second.
- Open on the shared moment, not on her. The line, the wait, the pastry case,
  the thing she's holding.
- Say the observation and then **stop**. Do not explain the observation. The
  silence after it is the invitation, and it is also your deliberate-pause rep.
- Warm face *before* the first word, not after. Cold face on a warm line is
  the single most-repeated tell in your file.
**In the pocket:** *"I've been standing here trying to decide if the pistachio
thing is a breakfast or a dessert. I think I've decided it's neither."*
**Coach's eye:** the first eight words, filler in the opening sentence, whether
the opener's ending falls or rises, and smile onset relative to first word.
**Context tag:** `small-talk`

### C2 · The Object in Her Hands
**Where:** Book Loft, German Village. Narrow aisle, two people wide.
**Moment:** She's holding something specific — a book, a record, a plant, a
weird kitchen tool. The object is a free pretext and most people waste it.
**Autopilot (scores 35–45):** "Is that any good?" She says "yeah, so far."
Thread over.
**The move:**
- Ask about the *choosing*, not the object. "What made you pick that one?"
  outperforms "is it good?" by the entire distance the rubric measures.
- Guess, don't interrogate. A wrong guess delivered warmly ("you strike me as
  someone who reads the last page first") gets you a real correction, and a
  correction is a conversation. A question gets you an answer, and an answer is
  a dead end.
**Coach's eye:** informational vs. emotional question classification on the
first question. Whether you got to layer 2 within four exchanges.
**Context tag:** `small-talk`

---

### C3 · Between Sets
**Where:** A gym floor, 6:30pm.
**Moment:** She's between sets, headphones half-in. Contested territory —
plenty of people consider this off-limits, and reading that correctly is part
of the rep.
**Autopilot (scores 25–35):** Hover. Or open with unsolicited form advice.
**The move:**
- Short, low-stakes, exit-ramp included. Fifteen seconds, then release her
  unless she extends it. "How many you got left?" then *leave* if the answer
  is flat.
- The skill on this card is **reading the no and taking it gracefully.** A
  clean exit scores higher than a forced continuation.
**Coach's eye:** talk-time ratio and whether you exited on the signal or
steamrolled past it.
**Context tag:** `small-talk`

---

### C4 · The Dog Park Cheat Code
**Where:** Schiller Park, Sunday afternoon.
**Moment:** Her dog has made the introduction. The pretext is free — which
means everyone uses it, which means the pretext is worthless by itself.
**Autopilot (scores 40–50):** Three minutes about the dog. Warm, pleasant,
completely forgettable. You leave knowing the dog's name and nothing else.
**The move:**
- Spend thirty seconds on the dog, then **pivot off it.** "Okay, but how did
  you two find each other?" is an origin story and origin stories are
  identity-level.
- The tell that you nailed this card: at some point she stops looking at the
  dog and looks at you.
**Coach's eye:** timestamp of the pivot off the pretext. Later than 60s = card
failed even if the score is pleasant.
**Context tag:** `small-talk`

---

### C5 · Seat Neighbor
**Where:** Row 14, 90 minutes to Denver, doors just closed.
**Moment:** The window is the first five minutes. After she puts headphones in
it's over and you both know it.
**Autopilot (scores 30–40):** "Business or pleasure?" — the single most
autopilot question in the English language.
**The move:**
- Trade first. Give something small and true about yourself before asking
  anything: it converts an interrogation into an exchange.
- Then go sideways: "what's waiting for you on the other end of this?" beats
  "what do you do" by a mile and takes the same breath to say.
**Coach's eye:** did you disclose before you asked. Novelty score on your first
question.
**Context tag:** `small-talk`

## ACT II — When It Goes Wrong

### C6 · The Thin Reply
**Moment:** You opened, and she gave you four polite words and turned back to
her phone. This is the most common outcome of a cold open and the least
practiced moment in your file.
**Autopilot (scores 20–35):** Panic-fill. Ask a second question on top of the
first before the first one has landed — your documented stacked-question
habit — or apologize your way out ("sorry, didn't mean to bother you").
**The move:**
- **One** more beat, not three. Give her something with no question attached,
  so there's nothing she's obligated to answer. Zero-pressure statements are
  what re-open a closed door; questions bolt it.
- If the second beat lands thin too, exit warm and mean it. "Enjoy your
  Saturday" with a real smile is a better outcome than a rescued conversation
  she didn't want.
**Coach's eye:** stacked questions, filler spike, apology language, posture
collapse in the four seconds after the thin reply.
**Context tag:** `small-talk`

---

### C7 · Caught Looking
**Moment:** She noticed you noticing, and now you have to speak or become a
creep by default. Zero preparation time.
**Autopilot (scores 20–30):** Look away fast. The look-away is what converts a
neutral glance into a bad one.
**The move:**
- Hold, smile, name it. Owning it out loud ("caught me — I was trying to place
  where I know you from and I've got nothing") converts the whole thing into
  self-deprecation, which is your strongest native register.
**Coach's eye:** latency to first word and Duchenne proportion in the opening
two seconds. Speed and a real smile are the entire card.
**Context tag:** `small-talk`

---

### C8 · The Group of Two
**Where:** A bar, standing, two friends deep in their own conversation.
**Moment:** Two is harder than three. There's no listener with spare
bandwidth — you are interrupting an actual dialogue.
**Autopilot (scores 25–35):** Address only the one you're interested in, which
makes an enemy of the other one inside four seconds.
**The move:**
- Open to the *friend*. Win the friend and the friend hands you the room.
- Be brief and give them a clean exit ramp — "I'll let you two get back to
  it" — which, delivered early, is usually what makes them keep you.
**Coach's eye:** who you addressed first, and whether your voice carried over
bar noise without going shrill.
**Context tag:** `networking`

## ACT III — Making It Real

### C9 · Three Layers, Ninety Seconds
**Moment:** She's engaged. Now the clock is the enemy — a cold conversation has
maybe ninety seconds of goodwill before it needs to justify itself.
**Autopilot (scores 45–55):** Pleasant surface ping-pong until one of you runs
out of runway. Nice, mutual, forgotten by the car.
**The move:**
- Follow the emotional word, not the factual one. Every answer contains one
  word carrying more weight than the rest — *finally*, *supposedly*,
  *embarrassingly*. That word is the door. You found exactly this on take 7
  last session; it produced the best moment on record.
- Do not shrink-wrap what she gives you. No "a little bit of," no "that must
  have been eye-opening." Documented, repeated, still open.
**Coach's eye:** layers reached, and minimizer language on heavy content.
**Context tag:** `small-talk`

---

### C10 · The Ask
**Moment:** It's been good and it's ending. Someone has to convert it or it
evaporates.
**Autopilot (scores 30–45):** "Well, maybe I'll see you around here sometime."
Nothing happens, ever.
**The move:**
- Certainty, brevity, downward ending. "I'm glad I talked to you. Give me your
  number — I want to finish this conversation." Then **stop talking.** The
  padding clause after the ask is what makes it read as a request for
  permission instead of an invitation.
- Your best line on record is eleven words with nothing attached to either end.
  This card is the same shape.
**Coach's eye:** word count of the ask, sentence-final pitch direction, and
whether anything got attached after it.
**Context tag:** `small-talk`
